    Performance Improvement Plan (PIP) Template

    A PIP done well is a support plan with a paper trail, not a firing formality. This template documents the gap, the standard, the measurable target, the support you will provide and the review schedule — the elements that make a plan fair and defensible.

    Employment law varies by country and state. Have HR or an employment lawyer review your process before issuing a formal PIP. This template is educational, not legal advice.

    Running a PIP fairly

    1. Informal first. A PIP should never be the first time someone hears there is a problem. Document earlier conversations.
    2. Describe behaviour and output, not character. "Three of five reports in Q2 were delivered after the agreed date" — not "unreliable".
    3. Targets must be achievable in the timeframe and measured the same way you measure everyone else.
    4. Support is not optional. Training, adjusted workload, more frequent 1:1s, a mentor — write it down and do it.
    5. Review on schedule, in writing. Weekly or fortnightly. Record progress honestly, including improvements.
    6. Be clear about consequences for both outcomes: what happens on success and on failure.

    Language help: "needs improvement" phrases. Ongoing check-ins: 1:1 template.
