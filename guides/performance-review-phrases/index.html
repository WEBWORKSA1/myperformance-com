    150 Performance Review Phrases, by Competency (and How to Use Them)

    Specific, fair, evidence-based performance review phrases for every rating level across 10 competencies — plus the formula that turns a phrase into a review that holds up in calibration.

    Writing performance reviews is the task managers procrastinate on most, and the reason is usually the blank page: you know roughly how someone performed, but finding language that is specific, fair and defensible for eight people in a week is hard. Phrases help — but only if you use them as scaffolding for evidence rather than as a substitute for it.

    This guide gives you a curated set across ten competencies and three rating levels. For the full, filterable set of 500+ (including first-person versions for self-evaluations), use the Review Phrase Library.

    The formula: phrase + evidence + impact

    A phrase on its own is an opinion. Attach one example and one consequence and it becomes a finding.

    Phrase: "Communicates complex ideas with unusual clarity." Evidence: "Her Q3 architecture proposal was approved by the exec team in a single meeting." Impact: "…which pulled the project start forward by a month."

    Every phrase below is designed to be followed by "for example, …". If you cannot complete that sentence, reconsider the rating.

    Results and productivity

    Exceeds: Consistently delivers ahead of deadline without sacrificing quality. · Absorbs additional scope without any drop in standards. · Turned a stalled initiative into a delivered result by breaking it into weekly milestones. · Removes bottlenecks that slow the whole team. Meets: Completes assigned work on schedule and to the expected standard. · Manages a full workload and flags priority conflicts early. · Delivers what they commit to and is honest about capacity. Needs improvement: Has missed several deadlines this cycle; realistic estimates and earlier flagging are the priority. · Spreads effort across too many tasks, delaying the most important work. · Starts strongly but struggles to close; finishing the last 10% needs focus.

    Quality of work

    Exceeds: Work is consistently ready to ship without rework. · Anticipates edge cases others miss. · Raised the team's quality bar with a review checklist that cut defects by [X]%. Meets: Produces accurate, complete work to the standard of the role. · Follows quality processes consistently. · Documents work clearly enough for others to pick up. Needs improvement: Deliverables frequently need revision before use; a self-review step before handoff is essential. · Rushes to finish at the expense of accuracy. · Documentation is often incomplete, creating work for others.

    Communication

    Exceeds: Tailors complex messages to executives, engineers and customers alike. · No stakeholder connected to their projects is ever surprised. · Written updates have become the team's template. Meets: Keeps the team informed of progress and blockers. · Writes well-organised, actionable messages. · Adapts style to the audience. Needs improvement: Leaves stakeholders guessing about status; a weekly written update would resolve most friction. · Messages are long and hard to act on; lead with the decision. · Escalates blockers too late.

    Collaboration and teamwork

    Exceeds: Shares credit generously and raises everyone's game. · Built cross-department relationships that directly accelerated delivery. · Named by peers as the colleague they most want on their project. Meets: Contributes positively to team culture. · Supports team decisions once made. · Collaborates effectively across teams when needed. Needs improvement: Works in isolation; teammates are surprised late by decisions affecting them. · Reluctant to accept peer input. · Knowledge is not shared, making the team dependent on one person.

    Leadership and ownership

    Exceeds: Takes complete ownership of outcomes, including failures, and fixes them without drama. · Steps into leadership gaps and the team follows. · Two direct reports promoted this year. Meets: Takes responsibility and follows through. · Gives clear direction and checks in appropriately. · Accepts feedback on leadership and acts on it. Needs improvement: Attributes setbacks to circumstances or others. · Avoids difficult conversations, allowing issues to persist. · Needs to delegate more; taking on too much slows the team and limits others.

    Problem solving and initiative

    Exceeds: Spots problems before others see them and arrives with a proposed solution. · Digs to root causes so problems do not recur. · Launched [X] unrequested improvements the team now relies on. Meets: Works through problems methodically. · Raises issues promptly with options. · Knows when to solve independently and when to escalate. Needs improvement: Waits for direction rather than proposing next steps. · Addresses symptoms rather than causes. · Acts on assumptions without gathering evidence.

    Reliability and accountability

    Exceeds: Has not missed a commitment this year, including through team turnover. · Owns mistakes immediately and converts them into process fixes. · Trusted with unsupervised, high-stakes work. Meets: Can be relied upon to complete tasks as agreed. · Punctual and prepared. · Honours commitments to colleagues and clients. Needs improvement: Repeatedly committed to deadlines that were missed without warning. · Deflects responsibility when things go wrong. · Needs a systematic way to track commitments.

    Growth and learning

    Exceeds: Applied each new certification to a live project within weeks. · Asks for feedback constantly and acts on it visibly. · Coaches others on recently mastered skills. Meets: Receptive to feedback. · Keeps skills current. · Makes steady progress on development goals. Needs improvement: Has not engaged with development goals from the last review. · Defensive when receiving feedback. · Skills have not kept pace with the role.

    Customer and stakeholder focus

    Exceeds: Anticipates customer needs before they are voiced. · Turned two escalated clients into references. · Highest satisfaction score on the team while handling the most complex accounts. Meets: Responds promptly and professionally. · Handles complaints calmly. · Keeps customers informed. Needs improvement: Queries left unanswered beyond the service standard. · Over-promises and creates avoidable disappointment. · Prioritises internal convenience over customer experience.

    Time management and focus

    Exceeds: Protects deep-work time and produces more in four focused hours than most do in a day. · Runs the most efficient meetings on the team. · Eliminated [X] recurring low-value tasks. Meets: Manages time effectively and meets deadlines. · Prioritises sensibly and adjusts to change. · Uses meeting time productively. Needs improvement: Works on low-priority tasks while critical items slip. · Underestimates task duration, cascading into missed deadlines. · Needs to say no or negotiate scope more often.

    Language to avoid

    Character labels ("lazy", "brilliant", "difficult"), absolutes ("always", "never"), comparisons to named colleagues, anything about personality or protected characteristics, and feedback you have not given the person before the review. A review should contain no surprises; if it does, the failure is in the year's 1:1s, not in the write-up.
