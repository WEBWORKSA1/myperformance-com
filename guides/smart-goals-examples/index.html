    SMART Goals: 30 Examples for Work (By Department)

    Thirty copy-ready SMART goal examples for sales, marketing, engineering, customer support, operations, HR, leadership and personal development — plus the two words that make any goal measurable.

    Most performance goals fail before they start, because they are written as wishes: "improve communication", "be more proactive", "grow the pipeline". A SMART goal — Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound — is simply a wish with two additions: a number and a date. The examples below all follow the same skeleton, so you can adapt any of them in a minute. Or use the SMART Goal Builder and it will write the statement, milestones and scoring rubric for you.

    The skeleton

    [Verb] [metric] from [baseline] to [target] by [date] by [method], because [why it matters].

    Baseline is the part people skip, and it is what makes the goal honest: "increase leads to 180" means nothing without knowing you are at 120.

    Sales

    1. Increase closed-won deals from 8 to 11 per quarter by Q2 by adding two discovery calls per week and shortening proposal turnaround to 48 hours, because pipeline conversion is the team's biggest revenue lever.
    2. Raise average deal size from $14k to $18k by year end by attaching the premium onboarding package to every proposal above $10k.
    3. Cut average sales cycle from 62 to 45 days by Q3 by qualifying budget and decision-maker on the first call.
    4. Achieve a 92% renewal rate (from 84%) across my book of 38 accounts by December by running a 90-day-before-renewal health check on every account.

    Marketing

    1. Grow qualified inbound leads from 120 to 180 per month by 30 September by launching two webinars monthly and rewriting the ten highest-traffic landing pages.
    2. Increase organic search traffic from 40,000 to 60,000 monthly sessions by Q4 by publishing eight research-backed guides per month and fixing all pages with Core Web Vitals failures.
    3. Improve email click-through rate from 2.1% to 3.0% by end of Q2 by segmenting the list into three personas and A/B-testing subject lines weekly.
    4. Reduce cost per lead from $85 to $60 by Q3 by pausing the two worst-performing channels and reallocating spend to the top two.

    Engineering and product

    1. Reduce production incidents from 6 to 2 per month by Q3 by adding integration tests to the payment and auth services and introducing a pre-release checklist.
    2. Cut median pull-request review time from 26 hours to 8 hours by end of Q2 by assigning review rotations and capping PR size at 400 lines.
    3. Increase automated test coverage on the core service from 55% to 80% by 31 December, prioritising the modules with the most incidents.
    4. Ship the self-serve onboarding flow to 100% of new sign-ups by 15 November, raising day-7 activation from 31% to 45%.
    5. Reduce average page load time on the checkout page from 3.8s to under 2.0s by Q2, measured by real-user monitoring.

    Customer support and success

    1. Reduce median first-response time from 26 hours to 5 hours by end of Q1 by introducing a triage rota and templated replies for the twelve most common issues.
    2. Raise CSAT from 3.9 to 4.5 (out of 5) by Q3 by resolving 80% of tickets on first contact.
    3. Cut monthly churn on accounts under $5k from 4.1% to 3.0% by year end by launching a 30-day onboarding email sequence and a 60-day check-in call.
    4. Build a self-service knowledge base covering the top 50 questions by 30 June, reducing ticket volume by 20%.

    Operations and finance

    1. Reduce month-end close from 9 working days to 5 by Q3 by automating the three largest reconciliations.
    2. Decrease supplier invoice processing cost from $12 to $6 per invoice by Q4 by moving 90% of suppliers to electronic invoicing.
    3. Improve forecast accuracy from ±15% to ±7% for quarterly revenue by year end by moving to a driver-based model reviewed monthly.
    4. Cut order fulfilment errors from 2.5% to under 1% by Q2 by introducing barcode verification at packing.

    HR and people

    1. Reduce time-to-hire for engineering roles from 58 to 40 days by Q3 by pre-scheduling interview panels and introducing a structured take-home.
    2. Increase manager 1:1 completion rate from 61% to 90% by end of Q2 by publishing a 1:1 template and reporting completion monthly.
    3. Lift the employee engagement survey score on "I receive useful feedback" from 3.4 to 4.0 by year end through manager feedback training.
    4. Complete performance review calibration for all 120 employees within a two-week window in Q4 (last cycle: five weeks).

    Leadership

    1. Delegate ownership of the weekly operations review to a direct report by 1 May and reduce my own meeting load from 22 to 15 hours per week.
    2. Hold a documented career conversation with each of my 8 direct reports by 30 June, with a written development plan for each.
    3. Reduce team overtime from an average of 6 hours per person per week to under 2 by Q3 by re-scoping two projects and hiring one contractor.

    Personal development

    1. Deliver three presentations to audiences of 20+ by year end, requesting written feedback each time, to build executive-communication skills.
    2. Complete two 90-minute deep-work blocks on at least 18 of every 22 workdays for the next quarter, tracked in the habit tracker, to increase output on strategic work.

    Making the goal stick

    A SMART goal statement is necessary but not sufficient. Add three things: milestones at 25/50/75% so you know by week three whether you are on track; a check-in cadence with your manager (fortnightly is typical); and a scoring rubric agreed in advance, so "did I achieve it?" is not a debate in December. The SMART Goal Builder generates all three.
