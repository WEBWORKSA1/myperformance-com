How to use review phrases without sounding generic

A review phrase is a starting point, not a finished sentence. The difference between a review that changes someone's rating and one that gets skimmed is evidence. Every phrase in this library is written to accept a specific example: a number, a project name, a date, a customer.

The three-part formula

  1. Behaviour — what the person actually did (the phrase).
  2. Evidence — one concrete example with a metric or named outcome.
  3. Impact — who it helped and why it mattered to the business.

Example: "Priya consistently delivers ahead of deadline while maintaining exceptional quality" becomes "…this quarter she shipped the billing migration two weeks early with zero P1 defects, which let Finance close Q3 on the new system."

Rating-level language that stays fair

Notice that the "needs improvement" phrases name the behaviour and propose the fix. Avoid character judgements ("lazy", "careless"), absolutes ("always", "never") and anything you could not defend with an example if challenged. For serious cases, pair the language here with a documented performance improvement plan.
