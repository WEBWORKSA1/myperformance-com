Editorial Policy

Last updated: September 2026

Our standard

Every guide must help a reader do something better this week. We prioritise frameworks with research or wide real-world adoption behind them, give concrete examples with numbers, and cut anything we cannot back up. We write for both employees and managers and try to be honest with each about what the other side sees.

Research and sourcing

Claims about research, statistics or law are checked against primary or reputable secondary sources and linked where practical. Where evidence is mixed we say so. We test every tool and template ourselves before publishing.

Authorship and review

Content is written by the MyPerformance.com editorial team and paid contributors with management, HR or relevant professional experience, and reviewed by an editor before publication. Contributor bylines are shown where applicable.

Updates

Guides display an updated date. We review high-traffic guides at least annually and whenever the underlying practice or law changes materially.

Advertising and sponsorship separation

Advertising, sponsorships and affiliate partnerships fund the site. They never determine which topics we cover, what we recommend, or how we rate anything. Sponsored content is clearly labelled. Affiliate links are disclosed. Advertisers do not see content before publication and cannot request changes to editorial content.

Corrections

If we get something wrong, we fix it quickly and note significant corrections at the bottom of the article. Report errors via the contact page ("Correction to a guide").

Use of AI tools

We may use AI tools to assist with research, drafting and editing. Every published piece is reviewed, fact-checked and edited by a person, who is responsible for its accuracy.

Reader privacy in tools

Our design rule for tools: the reader's work stays on the reader's device. We will not introduce a tool that uploads what you type without saying so explicitly.
