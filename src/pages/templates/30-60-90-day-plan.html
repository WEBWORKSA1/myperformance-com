How to write a 30-60-90 day plan that lands

The mistake most people make is listing activities. A strong plan states, for each phase, what will be true at the end of it — and how you will know. Days 1–30 are about understanding: people, priorities, how work really flows. Days 31–60 are about early, visible contribution on something the team already cares about. Days 61–90 are about owning an outcome and proposing what comes next.

For interviews

Bring a one-page version. Frame each phase as a hypothesis ("based on what I know so far…") and ask the interviewer to correct it. The conversation that follows is worth more than the document.

For new managers

Phase 1 is almost entirely listening: a 1:1 with every direct report (use the first-1:1 agenda), and no structural changes. Phase 2 fixes one obvious friction. Phase 3 sets the team's goals together — see the OKR worksheet.
