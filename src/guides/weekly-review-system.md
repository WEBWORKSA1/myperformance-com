If you adopt one productivity habit, make it this one. A weekly review is twenty minutes on Friday afternoon in which you close the loops from the week, decide what matters next week, and write down what you achieved. It is the habit that makes every other system — goals, deep work, habit tracking — actually function, because it is the moment you look at them.

## Why Friday

Reviewing on Friday means you leave work with next week already decided, which is the single best predictor of a Monday morning that starts with deep work rather than triage. It also means your achievements are logged while they are fresh. Late Friday afternoon is low-value time for most people anyway; you are converting your least productive hour into your most important one.

## The five steps

### 1. Clear (5 minutes)

Get everything out of your head and inboxes into one list: unread email you have been avoiding, chat threads with open questions, sticky notes, the thought you had in the shower. Do not do any of it. Just capture it.

### 2. Review (5 minutes)

Look back at the week against what you planned. Three questions, written down:

- What did I finish? (Outcomes, not tasks.)
- What did I commit to and not finish? Why, honestly?
- What did I learn?

The first answer goes straight into your achievements log with a number where possible. Twelve weeks of these answers *is* your [self-evaluation](/tools/self-evaluation-generator/). The second answer usually reveals a pattern — the same type of task slipping, the same meeting eating the same block.

### 3. Check the goals (3 minutes)

Open your quarterly goals — [OKRs](/templates/okr-goal-setting/), [SMART goals](/tools/smart-goal-builder/), whatever you use. For each: current number, confidence 1–10, one action next week. If your confidence on something has dropped two weeks running, that is your first priority for Monday.

Glance at your [habit tracker](/tools/habit-tracker/) completion rate. Below 60% means the habit is wrong or the week was; decide which.

### 4. Plan (5 minutes)

Choose next week's **three most important outcomes** — not tasks, outcomes. Put a 90-minute block for each into the calendar before anything else lands there. Then look at the meetings already scheduled and decline or shorten the ones that do not earn their slot. Finally, write Monday's first action so Monday needs no decision.

### 5. Send (2 minutes)

Write a five-line update to your manager and anyone who depends on your work: done / next / blocked / need. This makes your work visible, pre-empts the "what are you working on?" check-in, and creates a written record that turns review season into a formality. It is the highest return two minutes of the week.

## The template

```
WEEKLY REVIEW — week of ___

CLEAR: (captured everything? y/n)

REVIEW
  Finished:
  Committed but not finished / why:
  Learned:

GOALS  (current · confidence · next action)
  Goal 1:
  Goal 2:
  Habits completion: __%

PLAN — next week's 3 outcomes (blocked in calendar? y/n)
  1.
  2.
  3.
  Monday's first action:

SEND — done / next / blocked / need  (sent? y/n)
```

Keep it as one running document; scroll up in December and the year is there.

## Making it stick

Put it in the calendar as a recurring meeting with yourself, same time every week, and treat it as fixed. If the slot gets eaten, move it — never skip it, even if it becomes ten minutes on Sunday. The review is where the compounding happens; a system without it is just a to-do list with extra steps.

After four weeks, most people report the same thing: Monday stops being stressful, because Monday was decided on Friday.
