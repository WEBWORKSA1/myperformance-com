Most deep-work advice is written by people who control their own calendars. This guide is for everyone else: people with a manager, a team chat, six recurring meetings and a job that is graded on responsiveness as well as output. The goal is not monastic isolation. It is **four protected hours a day**, or as close as your role allows, with the rest of the day organised so that those four hours actually happen.

Start by running the [Productivity Calculator](/tools/productivity-calculator/) — most people discover they currently get 8–12 focused hours a week, which is one or two productive days out of five.

## Step 1: Find the hours

Look at last week's calendar and mark every block of 90 minutes or more with no meeting. Most people have four to six such blocks a week, usually early morning and late afternoon. Those are your candidates. If you have fewer than three, the problem is meetings, and step 3 comes first.

Then note when your brain is actually good. For roughly two-thirds of people it is the first three hours after starting work; for the rest it is late afternoon or evening. Put deep work where the energy is, not where the gaps happen to be.

## Step 2: Book the blocks like meetings

Create recurring calendar events titled with the actual work ("Draft Q3 strategy", not "Focus time"). Specific titles are declined less often by colleagues booking over them and are harder to skip yourself. Mark them busy. Two 90-minute blocks a day is the target; one is the minimum.

The 90-minute length is not arbitrary: it is roughly one ultradian cycle, long enough to load a hard problem into working memory and make progress, short enough that you can defend it as "just an hour and a half".

## Step 3: Clear the meetings

Audit every recurring meeting with three questions: Is there a decision or a dependency that requires me live? Could my part be a written update? Could it be 25 minutes instead of 50? Then act:

- **Decline** the ones where you are an optional audience. Offer to read the notes.
- **Shorten** the rest to 25 or 50 minutes; the recovered 5–10 minutes between meetings is what makes back-to-back days survivable.
- **Cluster** what remains into two afternoons, leaving three mornings clean.

Most people recover 20–40% of meeting time in one audit with no measurable loss to anyone.

## Step 4: Build the on-ramp

Deep work fails in the first ten minutes, when the pull of the inbox is strongest. Design the transition:

1. **The night before**, write the first concrete action for tomorrow's block ("open the doc, write the executive summary bullets"). Never start a block by deciding what to do.
2. **Status notification.** Set chat to "Focusing until 11:00 — message me for anything urgent and I'll see it then." Colleagues tolerate silence when they know its end time.
3. **One tab.** Close everything but the work. Start the [Focus Timer](/tools/focus-timer/) — the visible countdown makes it easier to postpone checking anything until the break.
4. **Capture, don't switch.** Keep a scrap file for the thoughts that arrive ("email Sam", "check the budget line"). Write them down and return to the work.

## Step 5: Make the output visible

Protected time survives only if it produces things people can see. At the end of each block, write one line in a running log: date, what you produced. At the end of the week, that log is your Friday update and, three months later, your [self-evaluation](/tools/self-evaluation-generator/). When a manager asks why you were unavailable Tuesday morning, "I finished the pricing analysis you asked for" ends the conversation.

## A realistic week

| | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 08:30–10:00 | Deep work | Deep work | Deep work | Deep work | Weekly review |
| 10:00–10:30 | Comms | Comms | Comms | Comms | Comms |
| 10:30–12:00 | Deep work | Deep work | Meetings | Deep work | Deep work |
| 13:00–15:00 | Meetings | Shallow work | Meetings | Meetings | Shallow work |
| 15:00–16:00 | Shallow work | Meetings | Shallow work | Deep work | Planning |
| 16:00–17:00 | Comms + log | Comms + log | Comms + log | Comms + log | Comms + log |

That schedule yields 12–15 hours of deep work — double the typical baseline — while keeping every afternoon open for the meetings and messages a real job requires.

## When it breaks

It will. The rule is: a missed block is rescheduled, not skipped. If you lose the morning to an incident, take the 15:00 slot. If a week collapses, protect one block on Friday and restart Monday. Consistency over weeks matters far more than perfection in any one.

Pair this with the [weekly review system](/guides/weekly-review-system/) and the [habit tracker](/tools/habit-tracker/), and the schedule stops being a plan and becomes how you work.
