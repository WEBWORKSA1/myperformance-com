A performance review meeting has one job: to make sure the employee leaves knowing exactly where they stand, why, and what happens next — and agreeing with at least the facts, if not the conclusion. Everything below is in service of that. If the meeting is the first time the person hears a piece of critical feedback, the process has already failed; this checklist assumes you have been giving [feedback throughout the year](/guides/how-to-give-feedback/) and are now consolidating it.

## Two weeks before

- **Collect the evidence.** Their self-evaluation, last period's goals and results, your 1:1 notes, peer or 360 input, metrics. Read the self-evaluation carefully — it tells you where you and they see things differently, which is where the meeting should spend its time.
- **Draft the review** using the [performance review template](/templates/performance-review/). Every rating gets a specific example. If you cannot find one, adjust the rating, not the example.
- **Calibrate.** Compare your draft ratings with peers or HR so that a "4" means the same thing across teams. Adjust before the meeting, never after.
- **Decide the compensation and promotion outcomes** (or know that they are decided) so you can speak plainly about them.

## Two days before

- Send the written review to the employee 24–48 hours ahead. Reading it in the meeting wastes the meeting and puts them on the spot. Tell them: "Read it, note anything you disagree with or want to discuss, and we'll spend the meeting on that."
- Book 60 minutes in a private room or a private call. Not 30. Not the end of a Friday.
- Prepare your opening two sentences and the one or two messages that matter most. If they remember nothing else, what should it be?

## The meeting: a 60-minute agenda

**0–5 min — Frame it.** "You've read the review. My aim is that you leave knowing exactly where you stand and what's next, and that we've discussed anything you see differently. Where would you like to start?" Letting them start signals that this is a conversation, not a verdict.

**5–20 min — Their view.** Listen. Ask: "What are you proudest of this period?" "Where do you think the review is wrong or missing something?" Take notes visibly. Do not argue yet; collect.

**20–35 min — Your headline messages.** Two or three at most. Strengths first, specifically and with the impact. Then the development area(s), using situation–behaviour–impact, and check understanding: "Does that match how you saw it?"

**35–45 min — Resolve differences.** Where you disagree on facts, agree how to check. Where you disagree on interpretation, explain your reasoning and the standard being applied, and be willing to move if their evidence is good. Where you will not move, say so clearly and kindly: "I've heard your view. My assessment stays at 3 because…"

**45–55 min — Forward.** Rating and compensation outcomes stated plainly. Then next period's goals — proposed, not imposed; use the [SMART Goal Builder](/tools/smart-goal-builder/) together if useful — and the development plan with the support you will provide.

**55–60 min — Close.** Summarise what was agreed. Ask: "Is there anything you expected to discuss that we haven't?" Set the date for the first follow-up.

## Questions worth asking

- "What would have made this year a 10 out of 10 for you?"
- "What do you need from me that you're not getting?"
- "What's one thing you'd like to be known for a year from now?"
- "Is there anything about how the team works that gets in your way?"

## Mistakes that undo good preparation

**Recency bias** — rating the last six weeks rather than the year; your 1:1 notes are the antidote. **The sandwich** — burying the critical message; say it plainly, once. **Talking 80% of the time** — aim for 50%. **Surprises** — if it is new, it belongs in a 1:1, not here. **Vague development areas** — "improve communication" is not actionable; "send a written update every Friday" is. **Leaving compensation ambiguous** — if you cannot state it, say when you can.

## After the meeting

Send a short written summary within 24 hours: ratings, agreed goals, development actions, support commitments, follow-up date. Put the follow-up in both calendars. Then keep the [1:1s](/templates/one-on-one-meeting/) running weekly, because the next review is being written starting Monday.
